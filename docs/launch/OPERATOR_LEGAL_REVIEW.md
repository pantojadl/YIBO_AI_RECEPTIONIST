# Operator legal review

Drafts for the owner and counsel. Not published. Not a contract. Do not paste these onto a public site until they are reviewed.

## Decisions that need a person

1. Is the first customer a non-healthcare appointment business (barber, salon, detailer, cleaner)? If not, stop.
2. Which US state is the business in, and which states might callers be in? Recording stays off either way.
3. Who owns the phone number, the Google calendar, and the OpenAI account?
4. Is a privacy notice and a short terms page going to be published, and by which legal entity?
5. Are callbacks, reminders, or external transfers ever allowed? Default is no.

## Draft AI disclosure (spoken)

"Hi, you've reached [business]. I'm an automated AI assistant, not a person. This call is not being recorded. How can I help?"

The prompt compiler requires the AI sentence even if an admin greeting forgets it. Confirm the live audio actually says it before go-live.

## Draft recording notice

Do not use one. Recording is off. If a caller asks: "This call is not being recorded."

## Draft privacy notice (unpublished)

YIBO answers inbound calls for a business and can book an appointment. We store the caller's name, phone number, and appointment. We do not record the call. We do not want health information or card numbers. A live pilot would send audio to OpenAI and appointment details to Google Calendar if those are connected. To ask for a copy or deletion, contact the business operator. This notice is a draft and is not the business's privacy policy.

## Draft pilot terms (unpublished)

- Service is inbound appointment handling for one location.
- Not emergency services. Not a doctor. Not HIPAA.
- No guaranteed uptime, no guaranteed bookings, no 24/7 promise unless a human answers when the AI does not.
- Either party can stop the pilot. The operator deletes or exports the business data.
- Price is not set. Do not charge from this draft.

## Questions for counsel

- Does an inbound AI receptionist that discloses itself still need any state-specific notice in the business's state?
- If the owner later wants recordings, which states are all-party, and is a beep or a sentence enough?
- Does the FTC Act's deception standard require any change to the demo page beyond what it already says (simulation, not live, not HIPAA)?
- If the business is a sole proprietor, who is the contracting party?

## Sales answers (use these)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Are you HIPAA compliant? | No. We don't take healthcare customers. |
| Are calls recorded? | No. Recording is off. |
| Will callers know it's AI? | The assistant is instructed to say so at the start. Hear a live test before you rely on it. |
| Can I call a real number today? | No. The demo is a simulation. A real number is a separate purchase. |
| Can you call my customers? | No. Outbound is disabled. |
